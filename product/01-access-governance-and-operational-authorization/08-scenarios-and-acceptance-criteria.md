# Scenarios and acceptance criteria

These scenarios complement the foundation's general criteria in [14-acceptance-criteria.md](../00-andromeda-manufacturing-product-foundation/14-acceptance-criteria.md). “Deny” does not reveal data that allows content or the existence of a resource outside the scope to be inferred.

1. **Account creation.** Given Beatriz requests an Indústria Aurora account with a valid Owner, when onboarding ends, then an isolated tenant and traceable invitation/Owner exist; a correlated repetition does not create a second tenant or inherit data from another organization.
2. **Controlled activation.** Given a pending tenant and an unaccepted invitation, when someone attempts to register a machine, invite a user, or integrate ERP, then it is denied. When the Owner verifies identity and accepts policy, then the tenant may be activated.
3. **Owner is not an implicit operator.** Given an Owner without an operational assignment, when they open the Cutting Line HMI, then they do not see an order, signal, or action. Given a valid Operator assignment for C-01, then they see only that asset's permitted context and actions.
4. **Tenant isolation.** Given a consultant linked to Tenant A and B, when acting in context A and providing the identifier of an asset in B, then query/action is denied without confirming its existence; roles in B are not considered.
5. **Line scope.** Given a leader authorized on Line 1, when listing queues, alarms, and downtime, then they receive only Line 1 resources. An order from Line 2 neither appears nor can be prioritized, even on the same site.
6. **Temporary coverage.** Given a certified operator covering E-03 on shift B for seven days, when acting within the validity period, then they perform only granted HMI actions. On expiry or loss of certification, the next action is denied and the prior fact remains auditable.
7. **Production state.** Given an authorized operator and a planned order not yet released, when they try to start it, then it is refused due to state. When the order is released/prepared and asset, quality, and EHS conform, then the contracted action is recorded.
8. **Segregated configuration.** Given an engineer who edited/submitted HMI V3, when attempting to approve or publish it, then it is denied even with more than one role. Given a distinct reviewer in the correct scope, when they approve, then the version may proceed to publication.
9. **Protected quality.** Given an inspector who recorded an out-of-limit result, when attempting to release their own batch under a segregated policy, then it is denied. Authorized Quality contains and decides disposition with reason, evidence, and a link to the batch.
10. **Human delegation.** Given a leader who delegates queue coverage to a substitute until 06:00, when she prioritizes an order from the assigned line, then the action retains her identity and the delegation. At 06:01, the attempt is denied unless there is a new valid assignment.
11. **AI agent.** Given an agent delegated only to propose a downtime justification, when it attempts to confirm downtime or release material, then it is denied. When it proposes, then it references the HMI contract, asset, and policy; human confirmation remains separate.
12. **Restricted integration.** Given the ERP connector identity for order/actuals at South Site, when it attempts to read machine telemetry or send data to North Site, then it is denied. An authorized message keeps its integration, scope, origin, correlation, and result auditable.
13. **Immediate revocation.** Given a technician with an open session and a revoked maintenance assignment, when they attempt to attest asset return after revocation, then the platform reevaluates and denies it; prior activity does not become anonymous.
14. **Owner continuity.** Given a tenant with one active Owner, when an administrator attempts to suspend/remove them, then it is refused. When a new Owner accepts transfer, then the transfer records predecessor, successor, reason, and instant before removal becomes possible.
15. **Traceable exception.** Given an incident requiring emergency elevation, when policy approves minimum access for a defined period, then every use records a reason and a reviewer is notified. At the end of the period, access ceases and subsequent review remains pending until completion.
