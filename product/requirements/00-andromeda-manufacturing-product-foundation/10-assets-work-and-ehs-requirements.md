# Assets, People, Maintenance and Safe Work

## Availability is more than a machine state

A machine can be technically running while the assigned person lacks a qualification or a quality
restriction prevents production. Andromeda relates asset condition, maintenance, people, shifts
and environment, health and safety (EHS) information so the operation can understand readiness,
risk and responsibility together.

## Asset knowledge and maintenance

An asset's operational history includes hierarchy, criticality, location, capacity, manufacturer
and model, counters, condition, documentation, associated parts and relevant events or maintenance.
This information helps people relate an alarm or loss of performance to the resource's history.

Maintenance may be preventive, corrective, condition-based or inspection-led. Requests, triage,
planning, work permits, execution evidence, return to service and failure analysis remain related.
Unavailability, production downtime, maintenance work, parts and capacity effects describe the
same interruption from different perspectives and should be understandable together.

Completion of maintenance does not automatically declare the asset fit to produce. The applicable
release responsibility and conditions remain explicit. The product supports lifecycle decisions
that balance performance, risk and expenditure without prescribing every industry's maintenance
policy. [S10]

## Shifts, working time and handover

A shift combines a recurring template with a concrete occurrence in the site's operating calendar,
time zone and operational-date policy. Teams, roles, stations, language and local working rules
provide its human context. Planned time and actual work remain distinguishable, including overtime,
absence, extension and interruption.

A handover transfers responsibility as well as information. Outstanding work, order and asset
states, alarms, downtime, blocked material or quality, and safety risks remain visible to the
receiving team. The record identifies who handed over, who received responsibility, when that
happened and which exceptions were not accepted or resolved.

Night shifts and daylight-saving changes use the site's full IANA time-zone identity. Planned
duration expresses the schedule; actual elapsed time comes from instants. An abbreviation cannot
reliably decide the site's local time rules. The consequences for reporting and indicators are
explained in [Data and performance](11-data-kpis-integrations-and-governance-requirements.md). [S14]

## Competency and permitted work

People can be related to skills, training, certifications, validity and authorization for a task
or equipment. An incompatibility may warn or restrict dispatch according to policy. Local rules
can also account for workload or fatigue limits without presenting them as a single global labor
policy.

Work reporting and transfers preserve context and responsibility without exposing information
outside the person's scope. A team substitution does not erase the earlier assignment or the
qualifications governing the work performed at that time.

## Environment, health and safety

The product records hazards, risk assessments, controls, permits, administrative blocks,
incidents and near misses, investigation, action and follow-up on effectiveness. Each can be
related to location, asset, order and task so the people planning or performing work understand
its relevant restrictions.

Worker participation, hazard identification and continuous improvement are part of this product
purpose. The platform supports local policy and obligations without replacing emergency procedures,
safety devices, legal training or site stop-work authority. [S12]

## Priority and business impact

A production target or indicator cannot hide an active safety restriction, pending quality release
or unavailable asset. The conflict and the authority that resolves it remain visible. This helps
plants coordinate work with less ambiguity while preserving the reasons behind a delay, maintenance
cost or decision to stop production.
