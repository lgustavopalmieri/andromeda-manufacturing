# Product Priorities and Capability Dependencies

## Prioritize connected operational value

The foundation's priority is a coherent operational record that people can use and trust. Adding
an isolated screen or report does not resolve the problem if its material status, production
context, quality decision or ownership still needs to be reconstructed elsewhere.

This chapter explains relative product importance and capability dependencies. It does not assign
capabilities to a release or promise their current availability. The product requirements remain
broader than any particular delivery decision.

## Establish a trustworthy common foundation

Organization isolation, contextual identity and permissions, tenant branding, physical hierarchy
and shared master information support every operating domain. Auditing and versioned definitions
make the resulting decisions explainable. Calendars, units, reasons, shift templates and concrete
occurrences provide the common language needed to understand the same production record.

The versioned HMI/action contract is part of this foundation because it defines what an action
means for a person or a future agent. It does not confer physical command authority. Meaningful
indicators similarly depend on reliable event capture, shift allocation and declared definitions
rather than an early promise of enterprise-wide calculation.

## Connect the production journey

Assets and devices, their connectivity and state, configurable forms, setup and operator context
support everyday work. Manual entry, imported orders and equipment observations may coexist.
The product's value does not depend on every plant having the same automation level.

The connected journey relates enterprise demand and confirmation to planning and dispatch,
materials and consumption, process definitions, inspection, nonconformity, genealogy and indicators.
Versioned recipes, bills of materials, routings and reusable setup templates reduce uncontrolled
variation. Governed multi-site definitions allow reuse while preserving local responsibility.

A limited range of process families or connectors can make adoption easier to understand, but
that is a product scoping decision rather than a universal constraint on the platform. Broader
support needs to preserve the same evidence and authority principles across manual and automated
operation.

## Deepen the surrounding operational capabilities

Maintenance, packaging and shipping, competency, self-service reporting and corporate/local
libraries extend the connected record beyond order execution. Their value depends on people
being able to relate the new information to existing resources, products, history and ownership,
not on creating separate records that merely repeat those facts.

More advanced HMI behavior, alarms and trends, quality analysis, inventory and logistics,
environment and safety coordination, approval workflows and additional connectors can deepen
the same operational context. Adoption, data integrity and reduced reliance on reporting outside
the product are reasons to prioritize coherent use over breadth alone.

## Dependencies for advanced decision support

Finite planning and optimization depend on credible capacity, calendars, resource eligibility and
operational feedback. Predictive maintenance depends on meaningful asset condition and failure
history. Statistical process control and advanced analytics depend on reliable measurements and
the definitions needed to interpret variation. These capabilities should not suggest a level of
confidence unsupported by the underlying information.

Assistive AI similarly depends on explicit action meaning, trustworthy context, identity,
delegation and human authority. Autonomous decision-making is not assumed by the foundation.
The same requirement applies to proposed actions as to other operational decisions: the product
needs to make responsibility and consequences understandable.

## Boundaries and decisions still needed

Accounting and financial costing, complete purchasing or sales, deterministic controller and
safety-system control, full replacement of specialized warehouse, laboratory or maintenance
systems, digital twins and mathematical optimization of an entire supply chain are not implied
by this operational foundation. Their information may be related where ownership is clear.
Sector certification also requires a separately understood scope rather than a general claim.

Specific process families, sector obligations, connector coverage, local work rules and numerical
improvement targets need contextual decisions. This requirement preserves those questions without
inventing priorities for a particular customer or turning capability dependencies into a schedule.
