# Access, Accountability and Controlled Change

## The product need

Industrial information can have physical, commercial and quality consequences. An operator needs
sufficient authority to do the assigned work, while an administrator, integration or future agent
must not receive unrelated operational power. Andromeda connects access, history and controlled
change so the organization can explain both the decision and the authority behind it.

## Organization isolation and contextual access

Queries, actions, files, telemetry, notifications and information exchange remain associated with
the responsible tenant. Authorization applies before revealing content or causing an effect,
including access by direct identity or an alternative interaction channel. A person without
permission must not learn sensitive content through a denied operation.

Permissions distinguish action and scope. Temporary grants, delegation, expiry, revocation and
periodic review are part of access governance. Roles simplify administration but do not replace
context such as organization, site, line, asset, object state or an applicable segregation rule.

Human, service and agent identities remain distinct. Device and integration credentials have a
responsible owner, limited scope, state, expiry or rotation policy and attributable use. An agent's
ability to read an action definition does not grant authority to execute it. Delegation records
the policy, prerequisites, result and any required human confirmation, and remains revocable.

## An understandable audit history

Consequential actions retain who acted, tenant and object or physical scope, requested action,
previous and resulting information or outcome, time, origin, reason, approval and relationship to
the wider operation. Authorized people can investigate that history without silently changing it.
Denied attempts are recorded according to the applicable policy.

Changes to definitions, permissions, policies, master information, quality decisions, downtime
classification, integrations and HMI actions remain attributable. Execution retains its governing
versions even after a new definition becomes effective. This makes it possible to explain what
people were allowed to do and which information governed a particular decision.

Where an electronic signature or attestation is required, its meaning matters as much as the
signatory's name. The record relates the person, signed content, purpose of the act, time and
underlying operation. Sector-specific obligations need their own product definition; the existence
of a signature capability does not automatically establish regulatory compliance.

## Govern operational changes

A configuration moves through preparation, review, approval, publication or effectiveness,
replacement and controlled reversal according to policy. Higher-risk changes require an
understanding of their consequences, suitable approval and an agreed way to recover from an
unsuitable change.

Forms, rules and HMI behavior can be explored or simulated before publication. A started order
preserves the version initially applied to its execution. An explicit, authorized policy may
permit a live adjustment, but that adjustment is recorded alongside the original reference,
without replacing or erasing it. The product makes the adjustment, authority and consequences visible.

Tenant branding and local variants inherit only the adaptations permitted by the governing
standard. Their differences and responsible people remain identifiable, so corporate reuse does
not produce ungoverned local copies or expose another organization's brand or conventions.

## Operational technology responsibility

Operational technology (OT) connects information with the physical process. Availability,
reliability and physical safety influence how people assess risk and responsibility among asset
owner, supplier and integrator. The referenced ISA/IEC 62443 and NIST SP 800-82 materials inform
that context; this requirement does not itself declare certification or compliance. [S07] [S08]

## Business value

The organization can explain who changed an important definition, who released a lot, why an
exception was allowed and what the operation looked like at that time. Controlled access reduces
unintended exposure, while visible change history supports investigation and continued operation
without treating convenience as unrestricted authority.
